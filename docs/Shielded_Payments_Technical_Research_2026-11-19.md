Source dates appear in citations. “Undated; checked 2026-11-19” means the primary source supplies no reliable publication date; it does not mean the feature was introduced on the access date. Historical papers are used for historical measurements, not as evidence of current attacks or current throughput.

Source repositories and official documentation were read alongside papers and audit reports. A repository license is not a patent clearance or a license for every dependency. ### Executive decision

**Recommendation: prototype a single-asset, Sapling-lineage Groth16 shielded note pool with a small, explicitly new conditional-spend circuit, BLS12-381 verification accelerated through pinned Polkadot SDK host functions, and no account-signed payment extrinsics.** Keep contacts and channel authorization entirely client-side. Put escrow authority, refund rules and confidential fee accounting inside the proved state transition. Use one padded transaction envelope and client-side scanning. This recommendation is conditional on the feasibility gates at the end, especially phone proving, confidential burning, and a reviewable circuit specification. It is not a claim that stock Sapling, MASP or an arkworks crate already implements the required application.

**The strongest argument against that recommendation is the amount of new security-critical protocol work.** The escrow, common transaction shape, fee/burn accounting and integration would lose the benefit of treating an upstream circuit as unchanged. They require fresh audits and a fresh circuit-specific Groth16 phase 2. A setup-free, current Orchard/Ironwood-based implementation could be preferable if a pinned Halo 2 verifier and the required circuit extensions meet the phone and block budgets. The comparative case and evidence gaps are developed below; do not decide from proof size alone.

The requested literal guarantee—an observer of the entire network and chain, running an unspecified number of nodes, “must not be able to tell”—cannot responsibly be promised. A release needs a bounded adversary model, cryptographic assumptions, traffic assumptions and a measured leakage budget. If only one honest payer and one plausible payee exist, or if the adversary controls a counterparty/device, encryption cannot manufacture uncertainty. This is a logical limitation, not a measured attack rate. The relevant communication research also conditions its guarantees on honest participants and providers. [R01; 2017-08, §2.2–2.3](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity17/sec17-piotrowska.pdf)

### Three corrections to the starting approach

1. **Hiding transfers does not automatically hide the application.** A public `request_contact` call, a special 427-byte attachment, a different fee class, an escrow-specific nullifier namespace, an exact 24-hour refund rhythm, or a public invite-validation key can identify contact activity even if the payment proof is zero knowledge. This is analysis of the supplied design, 2026-11-19. All these observables belong in the privacy specification.

2. **A public percentage burn is a value oracle.** If a refund burns `b = floor(v / 100)` base units and `b` is individually observable, then `100b ≤ v ≤ 100b + 99`. Publishing the change in total issuance or the public pool balance has the same problem as publishing the burn argument. This is exact arithmetic under that rounding rule, 2026-11-19. A committed burn, enforceably unspendable value, or delayed aggregation changes the accounting model; merely removing an event does not solve it.

3. **Noise plus a mixnet is not the whole privacy protocol.** Noise XX provides authenticated key exchange only after the application correctly authenticates the exchanged keys; its specification explicitly separates handshake identity hiding from IP addresses, metadata and traffic analysis. Nym's current limitations document says destinations still see request contents and patterns, and that sustained bulk flows have weaker protection with correlation an open question. [R02; 2018-07-11, §§7.8,13–14](https://noiseprotocol.org/noise.html); [R03; updated 2026-11-17](https://nym.com/docs/developers/limitations)

### Precisely what is being protected

For this report, “hidden sender/recipient/amount” means that the public transaction contains no spending account, recipient address or principal amount, and that proofs hide note openings and spent-leaf selection. It does not mean a payment is invisible, that the sender cannot identify the recipient they chose, or that a recipient can never disclose a payment. The network layer must separately prevent linking a public transaction or a sequence of private queries to an IP address or stable application identity. These are proposed requirements, 2026-11-19.

The adversary model should explicitly state: maximum malicious mix/validator/RPC/relayer share; whether a gateway may be malicious; whether active dropping, delaying, replay, eclipse and Sybil attacks are included; minimum concurrently online honest clients; permitted side information about recipient prices and contact-directory identities; and whether post-quantum confidentiality is a launch requirement. The original Loopix model bounds compromised clients and conditions receiver unobservability on an honest provider. Treat the application's stronger requirements as a separate proof and evaluation task. [R01; 2017-08, §2.2–2.3](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity17/sec17-piotrowska.pdf)

**Source conflict to preserve:** Nym's Loopix overview uses very strong language about a global observer gaining no statistical advantage; its limitations page says sustained-flow correlation remains an open question, and the underlying paper has explicit assumptions. This report follows the narrower model and workload limitations, not an unconditional marketing interpretation. [R08; updated 2026-11-17](https://nym.com/docs/network/mixnet-mode/loopix); [R03; updated 2026-11-17](https://nym.com/docs/developers/limitations); [R01; 2017-08](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity17/sec17-piotrowska.pdf)

### 1.4 Material conflicts and version traps

**Orchard's audit history must not be presented as uninterrupted assurance.** ZIP 257 records a soundness vulnerability reported 2026-05-29, temporary disabling of Orchard, and replacement of the affected scalar-multiplication gadget and verifying key. Original NU5-era audits therefore cannot be cited as audits of the later corrected circuit. QEDIT's separate review covered the proof system, protocol, primitives, keys and circuit, explicitly excluding unrelated chain components. Ironwood activated 2026-07-28 and reuses the Orchard protocol with a new pool/state boundary; the old pool is no longer the normal destination for new private payments. [D05; created 2026-06-09](https://zips.z.cash/zip-0257), [D44; historical NU5 audit, page undated, inspected 2026-11-19](https://qed-it.com/services/audits/zcash-nu5/), [D06; activation 2026-07-28](https://z.cash/upgrade/nu6-3/), [D42; created 2026-06-13](https://zips.z.cash/zip-0229).

**Formal-verification evidence has inconsistent summaries.** The upgrade page calls Ironwood formally verified; the Ironwood Book landing page still says verification is in progress and emphasizes exact scope. This report does not infer full protocol, implementation, encryption or wallet verification from that marketing phrase. Require the checked Lean build, theorem/assumption inventory, extraction/fixture bindings to the deployed circuit, and an explicit account of excluded components before using a stronger label. [D06; inspected 2026-11-19](https://z.cash/upgrade/nu6-3/), [D43; 2026 project, inspected 2026-11-19](https://zcash.github.io/ironwood/).

**SP1 setup language conflicts across official pages.** The proof-types page says PLONK does not require a trusted setup while also referring to Ignition; the security-model page explicitly says both protocols require one and distinguishes the universal PLONK SRS from a circuit-specific Groth16 ceremony. The technically correct table interpretation is **no new per-application ceremony for PLONK**, not “no toxic-waste assumption.” [D27; inspected 2026-11-19](https://docs.succinct.xyz/docs/sp1/generating-proofs/proof-types), [D26; inspected 2026-11-19](https://docs.succinct.xyz/docs/sp1/security/security-model).

**Decision implication, dated 2026-11-19:** For this small-team, phone-targeted, single-coin system, a narrow Groth16 note-transition relation is the most plausible first feasibility candidate because proof verification and proof bytes are bounded tightly; however it creates a new audited-protocol obligation and trusted-setup lifecycle. Orchard/IPA is the strongest alternative if ceremony avoidance is a hard requirement, but requires proving that the runtime verifier and phone prover fit the actual budget. Neither zkVM should be selected on the claim that normal Rust code removes cryptographic design work or that outsourced proving preserves confidentiality. This paragraph is an engineering recommendation, not a measured result.

The ChainSafe benchmark is also preserved in its official repository report, including the exact proving table and hardware details. The original article intermittently returned HTTP 502 during cross-checking; the repository was retrieved successfully. [D45; 2024 study, repository inspected 2026-11-19](https://github.com/ChainSafe/zcash-wasm-benchmark/blob/main/REPORT.md)

## 2. Verifying proofs in this chain's runtime

**Conclusion (engineering assessment, 2026-11-19): Groth16 verification is a practical Substrate integration project; an Orchard Halo 2 verifier is a feasibility experiment, not an available drop-in FRAME dependency.** A standalone chain can add versioned native host functions without waiting for Polkadot governance. That choice moves consensus-critical code into the node binary and requires coordinated node upgrades. This conclusion follows from the SDK's runtime-interface architecture and the existing zkVerify and Midnight implementations; it does not assert that a particular copied verifier is sufficiently audited for this application. [S01; SDK tag 2026-10-12](https://github.com/paritytech/polkadot-sdk/blob/polkadot-stable2609/substrate/primitives/crypto/ec-utils/src/lib.rs), [S07; inspected 2026-11-19](https://github.com/zkVerify/zkVerify/blob/main/native/src/groth16.rs), [S27; inspected 2026-11-19](https://github.com/midnightntwrk/midnight-node/blob/main/node/src/service.rs).

### 2.1 What actually exists in stable2609

The inspected **`polkadot-stable2609` tag**, rather than an unpinned `master`, contains `sp-crypto-ec-utils` **0.24.0**, a `no_std` compatibility layer using `arkworks-extensions`. Its features include BLS12-377, BLS12-381, BW6-761, Ed-on-BLS12-377, Bandersnatch, Pallas and Vesta. `HostFunctionsRfc163` groups **BLS12-381, Bandersnatch, Pallas and Vesta**. **BN254 is not in this crate's exported module/feature list**, even though the separate arkworks-extensions repository contains a BN254 extension. Do not confuse a curve library, an SDK host interface, and a host function registered by a particular node. [S01; tag 2026-10-12](https://github.com/paritytech/polkadot-sdk/blob/polkadot-stable2609/substrate/primitives/crypto/ec-utils/src/lib.rs), [S02; tag 2026-10-12](https://github.com/paritytech/polkadot-sdk/blob/polkadot-stable2609/substrate/primitives/crypto/ec-utils/Cargo.toml), [S03; inspected 2026-11-19](https://github.com/paritytech/arkworks-extensions).

RFC-0163 is **approved**, started **2026-01-21**. It specifies MSM/scalar/pairing operations. Host decoding omits **prime-subgroup checks**; preserve these at the verifier's untrusted-input boundary. The RFC also explicitly says Arkworks lacks a formal security audit. Approval is not an audit. [S04; 2026-01-21, current text inspected 2026-11-19](https://polkadot-fellows.github.io/RFCs/approved/0163-ec-host-functions.html).

The same tag's Cumulus service already composes `HostFunctionsRfc163` into `ParachainHostFunctions`. The generic `sp_io::SubstrateHostFunctions` tuple does not itself contain that extra tuple. For **this standalone chain**, inspect and explicitly configure the node executor, benchmark runner, import path and any light-client executor that must replay the runtime; do not infer support from Cumulus registration. [S05; tag 2026-10-12](https://github.com/paritytech/polkadot-sdk/blob/polkadot-stable2609/cumulus/client/service/src/lib.rs), [S06; tag 2026-10-12](https://github.com/paritytech/polkadot-sdk/blob/polkadot-stable2609/substrate/primitives/io/src/lib.rs).

**Weight implications (derived engineering guidance, 2026-11-19).** At eight public inputs, those newer formulas give approximately **3.77 ms BN254** or **5.18 ms BLS12-381**, before the shielded pallet's remaining work. A hypothetical 0.5-second verification budget would accommodate fewer than 132 or 96 such checks once other costs are included; this is an arithmetic capacity illustration, **not a TPS forecast**. Benchmark the chosen validator reference hardware and exact node build. Charge bounded proof decoding, subgroup checks, invalid proofs, commitments/Merkle updates, nullifier reads/writes, ciphertext handling and worst-case historic-root access. **PolkaVM distinction.** stable2609 includes a PolkaVM runtime executor with host-function plumbing; the inspected implementation explicitly says instruction gas metering is not enabled in that runtime execution path. This does not exempt FRAME calls from weight accounting. `pallet-revive` executing Solidity/RISC-V contracts is a different execution layer from the chain runtime itself. No directly comparable Groth16/Orchard Wasm-versus-PolkaVM full-verification measurement was located. Treat switching the whole chain runtime VM as a separate integration/benchmark project, not a promised cryptographic speedup. [S17; tag 2026-10-12](https://github.com/paritytech/polkadot-sdk/blob/polkadot-stable2609/substrate/client/executor/polkavm/src/lib.rs), [S18; official docs inspected 2026-11-19](https://docs.polkadot.com/smart-contracts/for-eth-devs/evm-vs-pvm/).

### 4.2 A minimal statement for this application

**Proposed design, 2026-11-19; unaudited.** Use one note system with ordinary, escrow and provably unspendable accounting-note types. A private escrow plaintext commits to `(chain_id, version, asset, value, recipient_authority, sender_refund_destination, sender_cancel_authority, policy, nonce)`; the runtime's leaf construction also authenticates its creation time. Do not publish an escrow ID, recipient key, channel ID, refund address, accepted flag or deadline unique to that note. Use one indistinguishable public transaction envelope and a proof that selects the permitted branch privately. A special public `accept_request` extrinsic would identify request resolutions even if it hid addresses.

**Proposed state machine, 2026-11-19.** All resolution branches must produce the **same deterministic nullifier for the same escrow**, independent of claimant, chosen branch or randomized authorization key. The chain rejects duplicate nullifiers across and within transactions. Branch-specific nullifiers would permit one claim and one refund of the same value. Every authorization binds the complete transaction effect digest, including output commitments, ciphertext hashes, fee schedule, validity bounds, chain ID and circuit version, so a relayer cannot replace outputs or replay the proof elsewhere.

**Required new escrow construction, 2026-11-19:** this cannot be obtained by giving sender and recipient separate stock Sapling spending keys and calling its ordinary spend circuit twice. The escrow must commit to a common nullifier seed/derivation that both authorized parties can use, while the circuit separately checks which authority permits the selected resolution. Both parties must receive the required escrow witness material securely. A reviewed, domain-separated derivation from a committed escrow-specific secret must yield one nullifier across all branches; changing the branch authority must never change it. This modifies the note relation/circuit and requires a fresh application audit and, for Groth16, circuit-specific setup. Sender knowledge of this common nullifier can let that sender recognize resolution of its own escrow; it does not make that transaction publicly linkable without the secret.

**Proposed constraint checklist, 2026-11-19:**

1. Prove membership of each spent note in an accepted commitment-tree root, using a private path and position; recompute the note commitment and correctly derived, domain-separated nullifier. Prove spend authorization using reviewed signature/key gadgets, canonical field encodings and subgroup checks where applicable.
2. Prove the note is the expected type and asset; range-check every amount and integer, constrain branch selectors to booleans, prevent field-modulus wraparound, and enforce conservation across ordinary transfers, escrow value, refunds, fees, treasury contributions and burns. Bind dummy input/output flags so padding cannot mint value or bypass authorization.
3. **Claim:** demonstrate recipient authorization; require current consensus time before the deadline if the policy makes recipient claims expire; force the designated amount into a new ordinary note controlled by the committed recipient. The recipient may need to fund an additional settlement fee unless it was prepaid at opening.
4. **Timeout refund:** demonstrate sender refund authorization, or prove that a permissionless claim sends all value to the precommitted sender; require current consensus time at/after the deadline; force `refund + burn = escrow_value` before any separately defined settlement fee.
5. **Early decline/cancel:** demonstrate the policy's required authorization and enforce the same refund/burn destination rule. A recipient can decline without learning or controlling the sender's spending key. Decide explicitly whether sender-only early cancel is permitted, or whether sender abort requires both parties.
6. **Burn arithmetic:** for a floor-rounded 1% penalty, constrain `value = 100 * burn + remainder`, `0 <= remainder < 100`, with integer bounds. This implements `burn = floor(value/100)`. If “at least 1%” is intended, use ceiling rounding instead. Tiny values can produce zero floor-rounded burn; choose minimum escrow values and specify the rule.
7. Bind ciphertexts to commitments and authorization. Decide whether the circuit proves valid encryption and availability for the recipient, or whether malformed ciphertext constitutes sender-funded griefing; a public commitment alone does not ensure a recipient can learn the witness needed to claim. The request ciphertext and any note ciphertext must be included in authenticated transaction effects.

**Why this deserves its own audit:** Penumbra's 2023 audits found an incorrect in-circuit viewing-key derivation that allowed multiple nullifiers for one note, and a separate duplicate-nullifier issue in voting. Fixes were subsequently reviewed. This is concrete evidence that membership proofs and a working SNARK verifier do not by themselves establish spend uniqueness or correct integration. [E13; 2023-08-21](https://penumbra.exchange/blog/2023-audits)

### 4.3 The deadline and automation traps

**Design consequence, 2026-11-19:** the prover cannot know a future block's inclusion timestamp. A private, sender-chosen `deadline` is insufficient: a malicious sender could choose an already-expired escrow. One viable approach is for the runtime to append `H(commitment, creation_block_timestamp)` to the tree; a later circuit proves membership of that enriched leaf, privately authenticates the creation timestamp, and compares `creation_timestamp + 86,400` to a public consensus-time validity bound. Every output uses the same leaf shape so “has creation time” does not identify escrows. An alternative is an explicitly epoch-rounded 24-hour policy, with opening transactions valid only in the designated opening epoch.

**Design consequence, 2026-11-19:** the runtime must compare actual inclusion time to the proof's lower/upper validity bounds. Proving “an old anchor was after expiry” can establish a lower bound if authenticated correctly; proving “an old anchor was before expiry” cannot guarantee a claim is still timely. Allowing proofs made before expiry to be included indefinitely afterward creates a race-policy bug. Exact wall-clock guarantees also depend on the chain's timestamp constraints and liveness, not merely on an 86,400 constant.

**Design consequence, 2026-11-19:** “refund after 24 hours” should mean *a refund becomes enforceably claimable then*. The chain cannot automatically find and refund an unidentified encrypted escrow without an authorized agent possessing the required data. A client or watchtower must submit a valid resolution; a fixed-destination, prepaid-fee refund can be safely delegated, but the delegate learns its schedule and possibly plaintext. Penumbra provides a specific precedent for permissionless/fixed-destination claim preparation using viewing authority, not for this refund policy. [E03; undated, accessed 2026-11-19](https://protocol.penumbra.zone/main/dex/swap.html)

**Selected policy preserving the requested unilateral cancellation, 2026-11-19:** either party may cancel early, and **first finalized valid resolution wins**. The sender can therefore race an acceptance until that acceptance is finalized. A common nullifier prevents double payment but cannot make both competing outcomes win. Requiring cooperative sender abort would reduce that race but would change the stated requirement, so it is an alternative rather than the selected policy. Offline recipient clients, censorship and validator ordering can affect which outcome wins; no cryptographic escrow removes that ordering decision.

### 4.4 Invites, blocks and recipient prices after moving state offchain

**Proposed design, 2026-11-19:** a cheaper invited-contact fee needs an unforgeable private invitation capability if consensus must enforce the discount. Bind it to a fresh sender authority and a recipient policy key, include expiry and a nonce, and prove possession/authorization inside the transaction without exposing the keys. A one-use invitation can carry a domain-separated nullifier; reusable invitations need different anti-replay and rate-limit semantics. A circuit-friendly signature implementation must itself be established and reviewed; putting Ed25519 or another existing signature into a circuit can be expensive, so benchmark the actual selected gadget rather than inventing a new primitive.

**Required invitation binding, 2026-11-19:** the invitation/policy signature must authenticate the actual recipient authority used in the funded note or escrow, the contact-encryption endpoint and applicable fee/price policy. The proof must bind that signed policy and capability to the transaction's recipient/output commitments and authenticated ciphertext effects. Otherwise a sender can self-issue a cheap invitation under an unrelated key while constructing a request/payment for somebody else. If malformed or misdirected encryption is otherwise tolerated as sender-funded griefing, separately assess that this tolerance cannot let a cheap self-invitation carry a costly stranger request to a different contact endpoint. Mere proof of possession of *some* valid invitation is insufficient.

**Design limitation, 2026-11-19:** deleting onchain contact state does not give consensus knowledge of a client's current block list or revoked invitation. Blocks can be enforced locally by refusing delivery/display/acceptance. Global revocation of a cryptographic invitation requires bounded expiry or an authenticated revocation/accumulator mechanism, whose updates and queries have their own metadata. A recipient's current minimum price can be an offchain signed quote proven privately; alternatively the client can reject underpriced offers, with consensus enforcing only the network fee. These alternatives have different spam economics. The report does not claim that client-local blocking alone enforces a chain-level payment restriction.

## 5. Fees, burning, relayers and public operator funding

### 5.1 No public user account is needed to pay

Zcash's balance fields and binding signatures allow shielded value to cover a publicly checkable transaction imbalance; a transparent fee-paying user input is not intrinsically required. Its fee policy explicitly requires fees to be computable from public transaction data rather than private note selection and supports padding without a fee penalty. Current ZIP 317 was updated on 2026-10-22 and distinguishes active from proposed revisions; this report does not assume one fee number applies to every current/future revision. [E01; 2021-02-28, accessed 2026-11-19](https://zips.z.cash/zip-0225), [E14; last updated 2026-10-22](https://zips.z.cash/zip-0317)

Penumbra explicitly encodes fees transparently while requiring all action value balances plus fees to sum to zero. Aleo documents private fees paid from private credit records and independently authorized sponsorship. These are examples of **payer privacy**, not fee-amount privacy. [E15; undated, accessed 2026-11-19](https://protocol.penumbra.zone/main/concepts/transactions.html), [E11; undated, accessed 2026-11-19](https://docs.aleo.org/learn/core-concepts/transactions/index.html)

**Proposed conservation rule, 2026-11-19:** `sum(private_inputs) = sum(private_outputs) + public_base_fee + confidential_extra_network_charge + confidential_refund_burn`. The proof establishes the equation, and the runtime directs the disclosed base-fee portion to its public burn/pool split. The confidential extra charge must be constrained to protocol-owned shielded treasury accounting and/or the enforceably retired burn type in the required policy shares; it cannot become an ordinary sender-controlled output or merely disappear from an unchecked accumulator. Avoid counting a treasury accounting output both in `private_outputs` and again as a separate charge. The user submits a self-authorizing proof-bearing transaction; there is no wallet account nonce or public wallet signature outside the shielded authorization. A relayer can forward the same bytes, or sponsor a distinct outer transaction, but its reimbursement output and every public fee parameter must be signed/proved so it cannot redirect value.

### 5.2 The 1% burn is not harmless public metadata

**Arithmetic consequence, 2026-11-19:** publishing `b = floor(v/100)` narrows the secret original value to `100b <= v <= 100b+99` base units. With ceiling rounding it similarly narrows a 100-unit interval. Publicly reducing FRAME `total_issuance` by that burn on each resolution therefore reveals almost the entire escrow value. Encrypting the output note does not repair this side channel. The same applies to publicly crediting an operator pool with a fixed percentage of a secret payment rather than a fixed network fee.

**Proposed choices, 2026-11-19:**

- Keep network fees fixed or in a small common public schedule, independent of payment/escrow value. A publicly credited fixed share reveals the fee class, not the payment amount. If invited and stranger fees differ publicly, however, observers learn the class. If that classification is unacceptable, charge a common public base fee and account for the private difference confidentially.
- Represent variable burns as a **circuit-enforced unspendable accounting type**, or update a confidential supply/burn commitment with a proof. Do not depend on “send to a zero public key” without proving unspendability under the actual protocol. Supply dashboards then report gross issued and cryptographically committed retired supply, not an exact per-transaction circulating-supply integer.
- If public burn or treasury totals are required, reveal only epoch aggregates, accompanied by a conservation proof. Avoid singleton/low-volume epochs, identifiable contributors and repeated overlapping aggregate queries; these can reveal individual amounts. Securely deriving an aggregate from many independent encrypted burn records is an additional protocol, not a free property of Groth16.

**Tradeoff, 2026-11-19:** the minimal first release can keep the 1% permanently retired inside the shielded accounting system, postpone exact public net-supply reporting, and audit this invariant. If the product insists on an exact public supply decrease for every refund, payment-amount secrecy and that reporting requirement conflict mathematically. Hiding invited/stranger classification while immediately exposing their distinct fees presents a similar conflict.

### 5.3 Substrate transaction integration

Current SDK documentation deprecates `ValidateUnsigned` in favour of `#[pallet::authorize]` with `frame_system::AuthorizeCall` and `TransactionExtension`; it also warns that validation runs before pool admission and repeatedly afterward and should be lightweight. The requested `polkadot-stable2609` tag also contains `AuthorizeCall`, its authorization-weight hook and tests constructing general transactions without an outer account signature. This verifies that this route exists on the requested line; the complete shielded authorization and fee extension still needs implementation and metering. [R09; tag released 2026-10-12, inspected 2026-11-19](https://github.com/paritytech/polkadot-sdk/blob/polkadot-stable2609/substrate/frame/system/src/extensions/authorize_call.rs) [E16; undated, accessed 2026-11-19](https://paritytech.github.io/polkadot-sdk/master/sp_runtime/traits/trait.ValidateUnsigned.html), [E17; undated, accessed 2026-11-19](https://paritytech.github.io/polkadot-sdk/master/sp_runtime/traits/transaction_extension/trait.TransactionExtension.html)

**Proposed integration, 2026-11-19:** perform cheap bounded decoding, length/version checks, accepted-root checks, nullifier lookup and preliminary fee bounds first; perform the expensive proof check in a metered authorization path; derive transaction-pool `provides` tags from the nullifiers. Re-check uniqueness and validity bounds against the actual state before committing atomic outputs. Do not accept unauthenticated arbitrary unsigned calls. Cache verified immutable proof/effect hashes at the node layer without making consensus depend on the cache; rate-limit ingress and relayers because invalid proofs cannot necessarily be charged after validation fails. Benchmark repeated mempool revalidation as well as block execution. A single global sender nonce would recreate account linkability.

**Relayer limitation, 2026-11-19:** relayers remove the need for a public user fee account; they do not by themselves hide source IP, first-seen timing or client-specific retries. Route submission and queries over the mixnet, avoid stable relayer accounts per user, and make reimbursement indistinguishable from ordinary shielded outputs. Outsourced proving reveals whatever witness the prover receives unless a separate private delegated-proving protocol protects it.

## 6. Finding your own notes

### 6.1 Detection, retrieval and spend-state synchronization are separate jobs

An anonymous output normally supplies a commitment, encrypted note material and an ephemeral encryption key, without a clear recipient identifier. A receiver tries its incoming viewing capability against the stream. Zcash's deployed `lightwalletd` architecture serves compact blocks for local trial decryption; ZIP 307 remains formally labelled **Draft**, despite implementations using its design. A specification's workflow and its standards-process status therefore need separate labels. [W01, created 2018-09-17; checked 2026-11-19](https://zips.z.cash/zip-0307), [W02, implementation interface; checked 2026-11-19](https://github.com/zcash/lightwallet-protocol).

For this chain, detecting a note must also provide enough authenticated data to update the commitment-tree witness and determine whether owned notes were spent. Fetching only the 427-byte request note does not solve those other tasks. **Design recommendation, 2026-11-19:** define a compact stream, encrypted payload stream, tree-update stream and nullifier stream separately; make their privacy and authenticity properties explicit. This follows the separation in ZIP 307, rather than asserting that its Sapling wire format can be copied unchanged. [W01, 2018-09-17; checked 2026-11-19](https://zips.z.cash/zip-0307).

| Method | Work and bandwidth | What a server or observer learns | Evidence and deployment boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Local trial decryption | Wallet processes every candidate in its scan range; compact records can omit large memo payloads. | A uniform download reveals a range and synchronization behavior, but does not identify successful decryptions. Selective follow-up retrieval can reveal them. | Used by Zcash light wallets; official interface and Foundation architecture description. [W02, checked 2026-11-19](https://github.com/zcash/lightwallet-protocol), [W10, 2023-03-01](https://zfnd.org/oblivious-message-retrieval/). |
| View tags | A short check derived from a shared secret rejects most non-owned outputs before the remaining ownership checks. A one-byte tag gives a random false match about 1/256 of the time. **It still requires computing the shared secret; it is not a 256-fold speedup of the whole scan.** | Properly derived tags do not publish a stable recipient bucket. For false-positive rate `p`, expected detections are `M + p(N−M)` for `M` real notes among `N`. | Server learns a superset of relevant notes, not their plaintext. Counts, intersections across users and active traffic injection provide additional information. | Published primitive; Penumbra has Rust implementation/protocol design, but that alone does not establish a deployed, audited outsourced-detection service. [W05, CCS 2021](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~imiers/pdf/fuzzy.pdf), [W06–W08, checked 2026-11-19](https://protocol.penumbra.zone/main/crypto/fmd/considerations.html). |
| Oblivious message retrieval (OMR) | Server homomorphically detects and accumulates relevant messages into an encrypted digest; the client decodes it. Server work remains substantial and is per recipient. | Under the construction's assumptions, the detector does not learn matching message positions. Connection timing and service use require separate protection. | CRYPTO 2022 construction and benchmarks; the Zcash Foundation's 2023 evaluation was research, not an announcement of deployment. [W09, 2022](https://crypto.iacr.org/2022/papers/530630_1_En_26_Chapter_OnlinePDF.pdf), [W10, 2023-03-01](https://zfnd.org/oblivious-message-retrieval/). |

Penumbra's S-FMD threat model explicitly allows colluding detectors and active message injection, and its parameter discussion leaves statistical questions open. Consequently **do not describe FMD as equivalent to full-download privacy**. In particular, repeated independent false-positive subsets can shrink by intersection; privacy depends on key/epoch design, traffic and the attacker's auxiliary knowledge. The latter is a design inference, not a measured break of a deployed Penumbra service. [W07–W08, checked 2026-11-19](https://protocol.penumbra.zone/main/crypto/fmd/threat_model.html), [W08, checked 2026-11-19](https://protocol.penumbra.zone/main/crypto/fmd/considerations.html).

### 6.2 Transparent scaling model for 10,000, 1 million and 100 million notes

**Calculations made 2026-11-19, not measured device benchmarks.** Let `N` be new notes since the wallet's scan checkpoint, not lifetime chain size for an already synchronized wallet. Use the user's **427-byte encrypted request note** as payload size. For compact-stream sensitivity only, assume **128 bytes per candidate**, an illustrative new-protocol budget, not an asserted Sapling/Orchard encoding. MB/GB below are decimal. Chain headers, tree updates, nullifiers, framing, padding, retries and proofs are extra. The underlying full-stream and probabilistic-filter models come from [W02, checked 2026-11-19](https://github.com/zcash/lightwallet-protocol) and [W08, checked 2026-11-19](https://protocol.penumbra.zone/main/crypto/fmd/considerations.html).

| Quantity — report calculation, 2026-11-19 | 10,000 notes | 1,000,000 notes | 100,000,000 notes |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Download every 427-byte payload: `427N` | 4.27 MB | 427 MB | 42.7 GB |
| Illustrative 128-byte compact stream: `128N` | 1.28 MB | 128 MB | 12.8 GB |
| One additional view-tag byte per note | 10 kB | 1 MB | 100 MB |
| Random false matches with a one-byte tag, approximately `N/256` | 39 | 3,906 | 390,625 |
| FMD false matches at `p=1/256`, `M ≪ N` | 39 | 3,906 | 390,625 |
| Payload bytes for those FMD false matches, `427N/256`, plus actual notes | 16.7 kB | 1.67 MB | 166.8 MB |
| Local scan at an **assumed** 10,000 candidates/s | 1 s | 100 s | 2 h 46 min 40 s |
| Local scan at an **assumed** 1,000 candidates/s | 10 s | 16 min 40 s | 27 h 46 min 40 s |

The tag and FMD rows have similar false-positive arithmetic but different bandwidth behavior: local view tags generally require downloading and examining the candidate stream; FMD lets an outsourced detector filter before delivery. The tag row is also not a count of expensive Diffie–Hellman operations avoided. [W04, 2020-04-14](https://github.com/monero-project/research-lab/issues/73), [W05, CCS 2021](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~imiers/pdf/fuzzy.pdf).

The OMR paper reports about **$1.02 per million scanned payments per recipient**; detection keys around **129 MB** for faster polynomial variants or **16 MB** for a slower TFHE variant; benchmark digest decoding below about **20 ms**. These are paper-specific measurements, not current quotations or phone benchmarks. Linear price extrapolation gives **$0.0102 / $1.02 / $102** for the three `N` values for one recipient. Multiply server work for more recipients. Digest size/decoding depend on message capacity, batching and variant; do not extrapolate 20 ms independently. [W09, CRYPTO 2022, §§6–7](https://crypto.iacr.org/2022/papers/530630_1_En_26_Chapter_OnlinePDF.pdf).

**Phone conclusion — engineering judgment, 2026-11-19:** tens of thousands of candidates are a realistic first prototype target; million-note restore needs measurement of bandwidth, thermal throttling, background execution and battery; downloading a 100-million-note history is an unacceptable default UX under the example budgets. A continuously synchronized phone processes deltas, whereas a recovered seed may require a large historical scan. No comparable primary benchmark was found that measures all four methods on the same current phone, chain encoding and anonymity policy. Make that missing measurement a release gate, not a guessed table of milliseconds.

## 7. Light-client privacy and malicious RPC servers

### 7.1 What requests disclose

The official Zcash wallet threat model documents bandwidth spikes when sending, fetching memos or receiving; a shared service can correlate two users' spikes. It also describes address-testing attacks by sending to a known address and watching wallet traffic, and the approximate wallet-birthday leakage from initial synchronization volume. TLS protects content on the path but does not remove these patterns. This document is an unusually candid source of limitations, not proof that every current wallet has identical behavior. [W11, official living documentation; checked 2026-11-19](https://zcash.readthedocs.io/en/latest/rtd_pages/wallet_threat_model.html).

The following are **protocol deductions for the proposed chain, 2026-11-19**, assuming a malicious node sees the application request after transport decryption. They must be tested against the actual client and RPC implementation, not treated as percentages measured in other chains.

| Query | Direct leakage | Recommended treatment |
|---|---|---|
| `path(commitment/index)` | Reveals interest in a particular output; correlation with later spends or multiple queries can narrow ownership and link wallet activity. | Maintain witnesses locally from a complete authenticated update stream; otherwise use suitably private batch/PIR retrieval. |
| `is_spent(nullifier)` | Gives the node a wallet-associated nullifier before or independently of public broadcast; repeated queries associate spend interests. | Download the common nullifier stream, or privately retrieve an authenticated status proof. |
| `get_ciphertext(txid)` after a successful local scan | Reveals which compact output was interesting; timing can connect receipt to subsequent contact activity. | Retrieve full epoch/block buckets uniformly or use PIR with padded request schedules. |
| Submit a transaction | Service sees exact transaction plus time and possibly an IP/session identity. | Separate broadcast from scan service; use anonymous submission and avoid stable account/session credentials. |
| Range sync, tree checkpoint or restore birthday | Reveals scan range, online periods and rough wallet age even when no individual note is named. | Common checkpoints, coarse start buckets, schedule padding; disclose the remaining leakage. |

These deductions are consistent with the documented selective-fetch limitation and RPC metadata exposure in [W10, 2023-03-01](https://zfnd.org/oblivious-message-retrieval/) and [W11, checked 2026-11-19](https://zcash.readthedocs.io/en/latest/rtd_pages/wallet_threat_model.html). A mixnet hides the route back to the client under its own assumptions; it does **not** turn `path(note123)` into a query whose target is hidden from the destination node. This last statement follows directly from the query semantics.

### 7.2 What is implemented, and what remains experimental

Do not freeze the survey at the 2023 conclusion that OMR was not a priority for Zcash. **By 2026 there is concrete PIR implementation evidence:** Vizor PR #601 merged on **2026-09-29** to recover protected Ironwood transaction details without exposing their transaction IDs; PR #971 merged on **2026-10-12** to integrate transparent-history recovery. The earlier PR explicitly says timing/query counts remain observable and adds no cover traffic. It also limits private recovery to the supported query domain; existing disclosures cannot be undone. [W12, merged 2026-09-29](https://github.com/chainapsis/vizor-wallet/pull/601), [W13, merged 2026-10-12](https://github.com/chainapsis/vizor-wallet/pull/971).

The wallet team's **2026-11-11/12** grant application describes released private queries as **experimental, off by default and mainnet-only**, with no silent public-lookup fallback. This is first-party deployment testimony backed by source PRs, not an independent audit or a claim that all Zcash RPCs are private. A separate Rust project, `vote-nullifier-pir`, implements YPIR retrieval of Ironwood nullifier nonmembership paths for shielded voting; that scope is different from scanning all incoming payments. No independent audit covering these precise PIR integrations was established in this review. [W14, 2026-11-11/12](https://github.com/Financial-Privacy-Foundation/ZcashCoinholderGrantsProgram/issues/107), [W15, source and API checked 2026-11-19](https://github.com/valargroup/vote-nullifier-pir), [W15a, checked 2026-11-19](https://docs.rs/pir-client/latest/pir_client/).

**Recommended first release, 2026-11-19:** start with full compact scanning and local witnesses/nullifier tracking; serve identical coarse buckets to everyone; fetch request-note payloads in common padded buckets instead of immediately on receipt. Verify finalized headers, roots and inclusion proofs, and handle omissions, stale snapshots and reorgs explicitly. PIR can later improve targeted data retrieval, but selection privacy is distinct from authenticity, freshness, completeness and availability. Do not adopt a multi-server PIR construction whose non-collusion assumption conflicts with the stated colluding-node adversary.

Bulk history downloads should not casually be sent through a low-bandwidth anonymity path. Nym's **2026-11-17** limitations documentation specifically discusses bulk synchronization, sustained-flow correlation and application-level request patterns. Prototype a transport policy that protects small broadcasts and sensitive requests while handling bulk common data separately, and evaluate the resulting timing exposure; selecting a fast transport is not itself a privacy proof. [W28, updated 2026-11-17](https://nym.com/docs/developers/limitations).

### 8.2 What these results imply for this messaging payment system

**Engineering inference, 2026-11-19:** removing public contact records is necessary, but the chain still needs to avoid publishing a recognisable sequence of “request now, accept shortly after” or “request now, refund exactly 24 hours later.” If each action has a different transaction shape, fee, ciphertext count or public escrow identifier, the protocol itself supplies a classifier even with perfect proofs. The historical evidence motivating this concern is behaviour-based attribution and public-boundary matching, rather than cryptanalytic failure. [A01; 2017-12-04](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1712.01210) [A02; 2018-08](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity18/sec18-kappos.pdf)

**Engineering inference, 2026-11-19:** the recipient-set problem is particularly acute here. An adversary can publish a contact profile, receive a stranger request, then know that request's sender-side timing, price and plaintext by being the intended counterparty. It can also send many requests to a target and watch reactions. Third-party privacy therefore cannot promise to hide a conversation from either participant. The model must explicitly exclude a corrupted counterparty's voluntary disclosure, while testing active probing of other activity. Loopix likewise distinguishes third-party unlinkability from knowledge available to conversation insiders. [A10; 2017-08](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity17/sec17-piotrowska.pdf)

**Engineering inference, 2026-11-19:** a pool's note count is not its honest-user anonymity set. A million commitments created by one tester or an adversary do not imply a million plausible independent senders. An attacker knows its own notes and can condition its inference on its own actions. Report both observable activity and an explicit adversary model; never display total commitments as an assured privacy score. Nym's own foundational description treats a broad shared user base as an important privacy resource, rather than claiming encryption eliminates the need for other users. [A11; 2021-02-26](https://nym.com/nym-whitepaper.pdf)

**Engineering inference, 2026-11-19:** Nym is a useful transport layer, but its presence is not an unconditional proof for the composed application. The foundational paper's route-compromise calculation depends on malicious capacity by layer: with three layers and independent selection, its example with one-half malicious capacity yields **0.5³ = 12.5%** fully controlled routes. That is a model example, not a measured present-day Nym compromise rate. The same paper says gateways see connecting client IPs. Application identifiers, query patterns and endpoint side effects therefore still need their own analysis. [A11; 2021-02-26](https://nym.com/nym-whitepaper.pdf)

### 8.4 Recommended launch policy for a very small network

The following are **proposed requirements, dated 2026-11-19**, not claims of deployed or audited protection. They respond to the attack mechanisms in the empirical sources above.

1. **Make ordinary money private from genesis.** Prefer shielded initial distributions and private change, keep spending inside one pool, and avoid a convenient public-payment mode that becomes the default. Treat bridges, exchanges, staking and node payouts as explicit privacy boundaries. This recommendation follows the round-trip and dominant-payout evidence. [A01; 2017-12-04](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1712.01210) [A02; 2018-08](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity18/sec18-kappos.pdf)

2. **Use a common envelope for transfer, request, claim and refund.** Equalise note slots, memo slots and proof/ciphertext lengths where feasible; use dummy encrypted data in unused slots. Hide the branch and avoid public request-to-claim identifiers. In particular, a 427-byte field present only for stranger requests would identify that action class; padding must address the whole transaction envelope. This is a design inference from the stated system and historical shape/timing attribution. [A02; 2018-08](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity18/sec18-kappos.pdf)

3. **Do not refund by a public cron job that names the original escrow.** Make the 24-hour deadline an eligibility rule and permit a later private spend; decide separately how a phone learns that it can spend again. The exact deadline, fee and penalty must not become a public fingerprint. This recommendation derives from public timing and amount linkability. [A01; 2017-12-04](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1712.01210)

4. **Pay fees from inside the shielded value balance.** Standardise fee schedules enough to avoid distinctive user-selected values, and do not require every sender to sign with a public account merely to buy block inclusion. Zcash's operational guidance specifically warns that public fees and metadata can create linkages. [A16; live documentation accessed 2026-11-19](https://zcash.readthedocs.io/en/latest/rtd_pages/privacy_recommendations_best_practices.html)

5. **Separate payment timing from messaging timing.** Prefund wallets; enqueue and batch background money actions; maintain a transport schedule whose observable behaviour does not immediately change when a request arrives or is accepted. Delays and cover traffic cost battery, bandwidth and responsiveness and must be measured on phones. These are proposed composition defences, informed by Loopix's analysed latency/cover trade-off. [A10; 2017-08](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity17/sec17-piotrowska.pdf)

6. **Keep the transport protection on for all wallet work.** Broadcasting over Nym while fetching a target-specific Merkle path directly by HTTPS would leave an application side channel. Scan, path retrieval, nullifier queries, telemetry and notifications belong in the same privacy review. Historical receiver-discovery attacks demonstrate why wallet receipt behaviour matters. [A07; 2020-08](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity20/presentation/tramer)

7. **Test hostile counterparties and hostile service nodes.** Include chosen-price requests, carefully timed messages, repeated requests and malicious RPC responses. Require that receiving, decrypting or rejecting a note does not cause a distinctive immediate public action. This is an engineering response to the active receiver side-channel literature. [A07; 2020-08](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity20/presentation/tramer)

8. **Do not market a small-set launch as complete anonymity.** Start with a limited-value experimental release, publish the threat assumptions, and state when timing/participation makes plausible alternatives scarce. Namada's explicit early-set warning is the appropriate precedent; its adoption totals are not a substitute for that warning. [A13; 2025-03-17](https://namada.net/blog/shielding-the-multichain-how-namadas-data-protection-works)

9. **Measure adversary advantage, not just volume.** Build a consent-based test population with known ground truth. Train an observer on chain plus ingress/egress records, then evaluate on held-out users and sessions. Record candidate-set sizes, posterior concentration, top-1/top-k inference, confidence calibration, false positives and success conditioned on adversarial participation. Treat the proposed release thresholds as product decisions backed by those experiments, not universal cryptographic constants. This methodology is a report recommendation, motivated by the denominator and ground-truth limitations in the empirical studies. [A05; 2024-08-09](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.05332v1) [A06; 2022-01-18](https://arxiv.org/html/2201.06811v1)

10. **Avoid treating reward-funded self-transfers as independent users.** If launch incentives are used, evaluate whether claiming rewards, reward timing, public budgets or operator-controlled traffic gives observers additional labels. No source reviewed here establishes a cheap activity subsidy that guarantees a meaningful anonymity set. This is an open design issue as of **2026-11-19**, informed by the incentive-side metadata observed in Tutela. [A06; 2022-01-18](https://arxiv.org/html/2201.06811v1)

The strongest practical constraint is a simple **deduction from the threat model, dated 2026-11-19**: if the observer can rule out all honest parties except one, a zero-knowledge proof cannot manufacture another plausible sender. Better cryptography prevents additional leakage; it does not erase external knowledge or create honest participation. Consequently, the product's defensible goal is quantified third-party unlinkability under explicit traffic and corruption assumptions, not an absolute promise for every payment from day one. The distinction is consistent with the explicit third-party and corruption models in Loopix and Nym's foundational descriptions. [A10; 2017-08](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity17/sec17-piotrowska.pdf) [A11; 2021-02-26](https://nym.com/nym-whitepaper.pdf)

## 9. Post-quantum exposure: confidentiality, soundness and recoverability

### 9.1 Do not compress three different security questions into “quantum safe”

Future quantum capability affects **recorded encryption**, **the ability to forge a valid spend/proof**, and **the information contained in old zero-knowledge transcripts** differently. Elliptic-curve public-key cryptography is exposed to a cryptographically relevant quantum computer; replacing key agreement does not automatically replace signatures, commitments or proof assumptions. RFC 9958 gives the current engineering overview and stresses that migration may require protocol redesign. [W17, June 2026](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9958.html).

| Candidate/layer | Confidentiality of stored notes | Balance, authorization and proof soundness | Historical proof privacy and status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sapling / Sapling-derived MASP | Classical curve-based key agreement is exposed when the adversary has the necessary recipient public address/key information. A public contact key used directly for chain ciphertexts creates exactly that exposure. | Groth16 pairings, curve signatures and curve-binding commitments require separate migration. | A soundness break does not logically mean every old proof reveals its witness. The complete pool includes encryption and public inputs. Groth16 paper and Zcash PQ analysis are the relevant foundations. [W18, 2025-03-31; checked 2026-11-19](https://zips.z.cash/zip-2005), [W19, 2022-08-18](https://github.com/zcash/zips/issues/1133), [W20, 2016-05-31](https://eprint.iacr.org/2016/260). |
| Orchard / Halo 2 IPA | Removing trusted setup does not remove elliptic-curve note encryption. The secret-address qualification matters here too. | Halo 2's IPA/discrete-log assumptions and Orchard's spending/commitment machinery are not post-quantum. | Analyze the precise implementation and transcript; “transparent setup” is not a quantum property. [W21, checked 2026-11-19](https://zcash.github.io/halo2/design/protocol.html), [W18–W19](https://zips.z.cash/zip-2005). |
| Custom Groth16, KZG-PLONK or IPA-PLONK | Depends on the separately chosen note encryption. A PQ envelope can protect payloads without changing the arithmetic verifier. | Groth16/KZG are pairing based; IPA relies on discrete logarithms. These soundness assumptions fail against sufficiently capable quantum attacks. | A replacement proof system requires new protocol analysis, including commitment binding, authorization and nullifiers. [W20, 2016-05-31](https://eprint.iacr.org/2016/260), [W21, checked 2026-11-19](https://zcash.github.io/halo2/design/protocol.html), [W17, June 2026](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9958.html). |
| RISC Zero's hash-based transparent components, without its elliptic-curve wrapper | Guest-program note encryption may still be classical. | Hash-based STARK components can plausibly resist known quantum attacks with suitable parameters; a Groth16 compression wrapper reintroduces curve assumptions. | RISC Zero's own security model separates STARKs from its non-PQ Groth16 wrapper and warns that a prover sees private inputs. This is conditional security, not blanket approval of every zkVM version. [W22, version 3.1; checked 2026-11-19](https://dev.risczero.com/api/security-model). |
| SP1 Hypercube | Encryption still depends on the guest application. Individual native STARK proofs are not presently ZK according to the security model. | **Even without a wrapper, the current memory argument uses an elliptic-curve multiset hash:** the security model specifies DLP over a degree-7 extension of KoalaBear. Its Groth16/PLONK wrappers additionally use BN254. | Do not label the current complete SP1 stack post-quantum just because it uses FRI/STARK components. [W29, current memory-argument description; checked 2026-11-19](https://docs.succinct.xyz/docs/sp1/hypercube/global), [W29a, security model; checked 2026-11-19](https://docs.succinct.xyz/docs/sp1/security/security-model). |

The **secret-address qualification is material**: Zcash's protocol author states that its privacy design is intended to preserve post-quantum privacy when addresses are secret, while issue #1133 seeks protection when addresses are known. Therefore the report should not claim that a quantum computer necessarily decrypts every historical Zcash note merely because it records the chain. Conversely, this system intentionally publishes contactability, so it cannot assume secrecy for the contact encryption key; keep that key separate from wallet receiving material. [W19, opened 2022-08-18; still open when checked 2026-11-19](https://github.com/zcash/zips/issues/1133).

ZIP 2005 introduces **quantum recoverability** for Ironwood: changed notes support a future recovery protocol if curve-based protocols must be disabled. It explicitly does **not** make the live protocol quantum secure; it leaves substantial recovery-protocol decisions open. The ZIP also explains that Sapling/Orchard note commitments are not post-quantum binding, so merely swapping the proof verifier would not restore balance security. Treat recoverability as a migration mechanism, not retrospective encryption protection. [W18, created 2025-03-31; Final and checked 2026-11-19](https://zips.z.cash/zip-2005).

### 9.2 What can be deployed using established components

NIST finalized **ML-KEM (FIPS 203), ML-DSA (FIPS 204) and SLH-DSA (FIPS 205) on 2024-08-13**. ML-KEM is key establishment; the signature standards are not interchangeable with anonymous spend authorization. The FIPS 203 page has a **2025-11-17 errata planning note**, so pin implementations and applicable corrections. [W16, 2024-08-13; note 2025-11-17](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/203/final), [W16a, 2024-08-13](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2024/08/announcing-approval-three-federal-information-processing-standards-fips).

For the public contact mailbox, evaluate an established **hybrid classical + ML-KEM envelope**, with authenticated version negotiation, ciphertext-to-context binding, domain separation and a reviewed combiner. The IETF HPKE PQ/hybrid document is still an **Internet-Draft**, last updated **2026-07-06**, rather than a final RFC. “Hybrid” in RFC 9180's title means public-key plus symmetric encryption; that title alone does not guarantee post-quantum components. [W23, draft-ietf-hpke-pq-05, 2026-07-06](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-ietf-hpke-pq/), [W23a, RFC 9180, February 2022](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9180.html).

There are production Rust transport components: released `rustls` **0.23.45** exposes `X25519MLKEM768` through `aws-lc-rs`; its `prefer-post-quantum` feature gives that group highest priority. The 0.24 development documentation has different default wording, which is why version pinning matters. This provides a practical TLS option; it is not a ready-made anonymous on-chain note format or a drop-in Noise XX replacement. `libcrux` publishes Rust ML-KEM code with a module-by-module formal-verification status, whose maintainers explicitly warn that proof coverage evolves. Check the exact portable/ARM implementation and build rather than claiming the whole dependency graph is formally verified. [W24, rustls 0.23.45; checked 2026-11-19](https://docs.rs/rustls/0.23.45/rustls/manual/_05_defaults/index.html), [W25, checked 2026-11-19](https://github.com/celabshq/libcrux/blob/main/libcrux-ml-kem/proofs/verification_status.md).

**An immediate sizing incompatibility:** ML-KEM-768 uses a **1,184-byte encapsulation key and 1,088-byte ciphertext** before encrypting any note payload. If **427 bytes is the entire permitted encrypted-note envelope**, even the KEM ciphertext does not fit. The design must increase/pad the envelope, add an authenticated sidecar, or deliver PQ-protected note data off-chain while maintaining reliable recovery. These are new protocol choices requiring review, not achieved by relabelling the existing ciphertext. [W16b, FIPS 203 Table 3, 2024-08-13](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/FIPS/NIST.FIPS.203.pdf); size comparison is report arithmetic, 2026-11-19.

Noise XX with X25519 remains classical: recorded ephemeral DH exchanges are exposed to future discrete-log attacks; classical forward secrecy does not solve harvest-now/decrypt-later. The 256-bit ChaCha key is a different layer and does not rescue a broken key agreement. Noise's specification allows protocol extensions but does not itself specify an audited ML-KEM version of this application's XX transcript. Nym's **2026-04-20** announcement described PQ exchange first for its VPN **Fast mode**, with mix-node channels and packet-format protection as later phases; it must not be cited as evidence that every mixnet packet and this application's E2EE are already PQ protected. [W26, Noise revision 34, 2018-07-11](https://noiseprotocol.org/noise.html), [W17, June 2026](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9958.html), [W27, 2026-04-20](https://nym.com/blog/lewes-protocol-post-quantum).

**Recommendation, 2026-11-19:** protect long-lived public contact-note ciphertexts with a reviewed hybrid envelope from the first release; keep crypto agility, explicit suite identifiers and domain separation. Independently commission a plan for future spend authorization, commitment and proof migration. Neither a PQ contact envelope nor a setup-free proof system makes the entire currency post-quantum secure. Do not outsource proving of secret financial witnesses to a server without a separately justified private-proving construction.

## 10. Keys and public contact identities

Zcash ZIP 32 separates hierarchical spending and viewing authority and defines diversified shielded address derivation. Exposing a viewing key is a much broader disclosure than publishing a receiving address. These capabilities should not be conflated with the messaging identity. [E18; created 2018-05-22, Final; current text accessed 2026-11-19](https://zips.z.cash/zip-0032)

**Proposed key hierarchy, 2026-11-19:** derive independent domains from a high-entropy backup seed using a reviewed KDF: shielded spending root; incoming and full viewing capabilities following the chosen protocol; contact authentication/signing key; initial-contact encryption key; Noise identity/session keys. Use existing protocol derivations rather than constructing new scalar/key relations. A public contact card authenticates its contact-encryption key and one-time/diversified payment endpoint, policy version and invitation mechanism. Never publish the spending key, full viewing key, nullifier key or a wallet-derived public identifier that reveals transaction membership.

**Privacy boundary, 2026-11-19:** a public receiving key is not automatically a chain link if output encryption is recipient-anonymous and commitments hide their openings. But repeating that key across services links those services offchain, and a published contact card links the messaging identity to its advertised payment endpoint. Use per-contact/per-request diversified receivers, no public receiver identifier in extrinsics, and separate public profiles from wallet backup/export material. A sender necessarily knows whom it chose to pay; a publicly contactable party cannot hide its published contactability from that sender.

**Disclosure design, 2026-11-19:** make a per-payment receipt or narrowly scoped record-view capability the default disclosure object; require a deliberate higher-friction export for incoming/full viewing keys. A long-lived full viewing key handed to an indexer defeats much of the objective against that indexer. Keep contact recovery separate from financial recovery: rotating the public contact key should not migrate funds or publish a wallet linkage; restoring the wallet should not automatically upload the contact book. Aleo's documented transition view key is one example of per-transition disclosure rather than blanket account disclosure. [E11; undated, accessed 2026-11-19](https://docs.aleo.org/learn/core-concepts/transactions/index.html)

## 11. A credible Groth16 setup for a small team

Groth16's practical two-phase workflow can reuse an appropriate Powers-of-Tau transcript, then perform a **circuit-specific** phase 2. `snarkjs` provides commands for both phases and for transcript/key verification. Reusing phase 1 does not remove phase 2, and a new circuit version requires newly derived circuit-specific parameters. [E19; repository, undated, accessed 2026-11-19](https://github.com/iden3/snarkjs), [E20; repository archived 2026-08-19, accessed 2026-11-19](https://github.com/privacy-ethereum/perpetualpowersoftau)

The Zcash Foundation's 2018 ceremony published parameters, transcripts, attestations and a verifier, with 87 contributions. Its statement is explicit: soundness relies on at least one participant destroying secret randomness; the protocol's zero-knowledge guarantee survives even if all participants were compromised. This distinction concerns a well-formed setup and the specified proof construction, not arbitrary malicious cryptographic code or compromised clients. [E21; 2018-04-13](https://zfnd.org/conclusion-of-the-powers-of-tau-ceremony/)

Tornado Cash's original project documentation records **1,114 contributions** to its May 2020 setup, and its public coordinator source documents local verification of the contribution chain. The documentation's claim that the participant count makes compromise “impossible” is stronger than cryptography justifies: independent honest entropy destruction and correct verified implementation remain assumptions. Pseudonymous participation can support a credible ceremony, but contribution counts are not proof of independence or secret erasure. [E22; historical May 2020 ceremony; repository accessed 2026-11-19](https://github.com/tornadocash/docs), [E23; repository, undated, accessed 2026-11-19](https://github.com/tornadocash/trusted-setup-server)

Orbinum provides a useful current counterexample: its official documentation says phase 1 reuses Hermez, but its current testnet phase 2 has **one contributor per circuit**; a public multiparty ceremony is planned before mainnet. A public beacon is documented, but it does not itself establish that the sole contributor erased its trapdoor. Do not describe such a testnet as having eliminated trust merely because phase 1 was multiparty. [E24; undated, accessed 2026-11-19](https://docs.orbinum.network/architecture/trusted-setup)

**Proposed ceremony checklist, 2026-11-19:** freeze and audit exact constraints first; hash-pin compiler, dependencies, circuit/R1CS, curve, parameters, verifying keys and chain configuration; independently verify the chosen phase-1 transcript's curve, capacity and format; run open phase 2 with independent contributors, diverse systems and optional anonymous attestations; publish ordered transcripts, contribution hashes and reproducible verification instructions; add an independently unpredictable final beacon under the selected protocol; have multiple unaffiliated parties verify the final artifacts and their own contribution inclusion; rehearse parameter distribution, rollback and circuit-version retirement. Audit the coordinator and conversion tooling when adapting between ecosystems. Freeze accepted verifying keys through governed upgrades rather than allowing a deployer to swap them silently.

**Cost evidence, 2026-11-19:** no trustworthy public primary-source price for conducting a ceremony for this exact circuit was located. Ceremony cost depends on constraint count, artifact size, bandwidth, coordination, security review and contributor support. The cited tools/transcripts are available publicly, but “no licence fee” is not “no engineering or audit cost.” Obtain a fixed-scope quote after the prototype fixes curve, circuit size and phase-2 tooling. An anonymous small team can recruit pseudonymous public contributors; it cannot replace external verification with anonymity or a large self-reported participant count.

**Setup-free tradeoff, 2026-11-19:** choosing an IPA/Halo2 or a genuinely transparent STARK route removes toxic-waste ceremony assumptions, not the need to audit constraints, transcript logic, verifier code, parameter derivation or upgrades. It can change proof size, proving cost and runtime verification substantially; those costs must be measured for the actual circuit. KZG-based PLONK/Halo2 forks and Groth16-wrapped zkVM receipts should not be labelled setup-free solely because the inner proof is transparent.

### 12.3 What to audit first

**Recommended assurance sequence, 2026-11-19:** first commission a written protocol/invariant review before optimizing or holding a ceremony: value conservation and private supply accounting; nullifier uniqueness; recipient/sender branch authorization; inclusion-time deadlines and races; fee-class leakage; invitation authenticity/revocation; note availability; upgrade and migration authority. Next audit the actual circuits and serialization/gadget boundaries, with independent negative/malicious-witness tests. Then audit runtime verifier integration, host-function determinism, weights/DoS, mempool behavior, replay protection and atomic storage updates. Finally review wallets, key derivation, synchronization, telemetry, RPC privacy, mixnet integration, backup and release supply chain. Repeat circuit-specific setup only after the audited constraints are frozen; retest fixes and publish scope/commit hashes before real-value release.

## 14. Recommended architecture and the decisions it requires

Everything in this section is a **proposed, unaudited architecture dated 2026-11-19**. It uses established primitives and published patterns; the combined protocol has not been built or demonstrated. Source-backed component capabilities and limitations are in §§1–13.

### 14.1 The architecture

Use one native-coin note pool, private by default from initial distribution through ordinary spending, contact escrow and operator rewards. Use a **Sapling-lineage BLS12-381 Groth16 construction**, with client-side proving in Rust and a bounded no_std verifier accelerated by the SDK's curve host functions. Select and audit exact arithmetic, gadget and verifier revisions; do not promote an unmodified arkworks prototype to production simply because it compiles. Reuse upstream code where the semantics remain unchanged, while treating the conditional-spend circuit as new. The small proof and host-function fit favor this initial candidate; they do not establish phone performance or circuit correctness.

The alternative worth testing alongside the first verifier spike is the **current Zcash Halo 2 IPA / Orchard-Ironwood lineage**. That test should be tightly bounded: can an unmodified, version-pinned verifier execute deterministically within the node's budget, through a reviewed host interface or a credible runtime build? It is a comparative feasibility experiment, not a second architecture to develop in parallel. Preserve the one-pool design whichever engine wins.

| Component | Proposed responsibility | Explicit trust/privacy boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rust wallet | Keys, trial decryption, local witnesses/nullifiers, proof creation, contact policy and encrypted backups | A compromised wallet can reveal money and contacts; no ordinary remote prover receives financial witnesses. |
| Offchain contact protocol | Signed price/policy cards, private invitation capabilities, block list, request delivery and channel acceptance | Consensus does not know a client's block list. Bind invitations and price terms to the actual recipient authority and transaction effects. |
| FRAME pool pallet | Accepted roots, commitments, nullifier set, common validity windows, bounded proof verification and atomic state updates | No user account signature, public recipient key, escrow ID, contact event or branch selector. |
| Versioned native crypto interface | Reviewed library arithmetic through the pinned SDK ABI; optional complete verifier hostcall only after separate review | All validators, execution modes and supported historical runtimes must agree; upgrades are consensus-sensitive. |
| Public-policy fee treasury | Fixed base-fee credits where public; confidential fee-difference notes and shielded operator rewards | Publicly identified protocol sink is an intentional exception to hidden recipient identity; exact confidential balance must not be exposed. |
| Scan/data services | Identical authenticated compact buckets, tree updates, nullifiers and padded payload buckets | Service availability is not trusted for authenticity; selective interests must not leak through follow-up queries. |
| Nym transport | Submission, sensitive queries, contact traffic, anonymous replies, retry and timing policy | Network metadata protection has its own adversary assumptions and phone cost; bulk synchronization needs a separately evaluated policy. |

Keep cryptographic primitives in established libraries. Creating application constraints with reviewed gadgets is still substantial cryptographic engineering; “no handwritten primitives” does not make new constraints reviewed. Have a specialist own the specification and circuit review rather than improvising new hashes, signatures, KDFs, group encodings or KEM combiners.

Selecting a reviewed verifier compatible with both no_std and the SDK's host adapters remains implementation work. Sapling/Bellman curve types do not automatically become Arkworks host wrappers; Gate 3 must demonstrate the selected conversion and verification path. A compileable prototype is not evidence that this whole path has already been reviewed.

### 14.2 One note format and one observable envelope

Commit the note type, value, authority, refund destination, policy version, note entropy and relevant commitments. Use ordinary, conditional and provably retired-value types **inside commitments**, with the spend relation rejecting prohibited type transitions. A hidden type is not enough if a public verifier-key selector, proof length, input/output count or ciphertext layout reveals it. Prefer a single bounded transition circuit with hidden branch selection and dummy slots. If several circuits are necessary, prove that their public envelopes are indistinguishable or explicitly weaken the action-hiding claim.

All contact requests carry the same number and size of ciphertext slots as comparable transfers and resolutions. The 427-byte request payload is an application requirement, not proof that a full financial note, its encryption overhead and a PQ KEM fit in 427 bytes. Define the wire format before committing to that limit. If 427 bytes means the entire envelope, standard ML-KEM-768's 1,088-byte KEM ciphertext alone is incompatible; if it means payload only, the padded envelope can be larger. [W16b; 2024-08-13, Table 3](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/FIPS/NIST.FIPS.203.pdf)

Public inputs should bind the chain and pool domain, circuit version, accepted root, nullifiers, new commitments, common validity window, public base fee and a digest of all authenticated ciphertext/effect data. If bytes are represented through a public hash, use canonical byte-to-field encoding and a reviewed collision-resistant binding; do not let field reduction silently equate distinct encodings. Enforce the exact public-input arity in the wrapper. A reported 2026 arkworks regression is a concrete reason to audit that boundary, regardless of which library is finally chosen. [D34; issue 2026-10-19, not independently executed here](https://github.com/arkworks-rs/groth16/issues/101)

Recipient-anonymous encryption is a separate requirement from ciphertext confidentiality. A hybrid KEM or TLS library does not automatically establish that a public ciphertext cannot be tested against known contact public keys. Commission a key-privacy and malformed-ciphertext review of the chosen note/contact envelope, including active sender probing, trial-decapsulation cost, version negotiation and constant padding. For launch, avoid writing sensitive human-readable request content into a permanent classical-only chain ciphertext.

### 14.3 Preserve the escrow outcomes explicitly

Opening spends ordinary notes into an escrow note plus private change and fees. A runtime-authenticated leaf wrapper binds the creation time to the inserted commitment. Both sides learn the escrow data needed to recognize and resolve it; neither receives the other's ordinary spending key.

Apply the same creation-time leaf wrapper and insertion path to every note type, so it does not identify escrow openings. Price quotes and invitation capabilities have bounded validity and an explicit rotation policy: a signed policy version alone does not tell consensus which offchain policy is current. Honor valid quotes for their stated interval, or specify authenticated revocation; local rejection alone does not revoke a previously provable discount.

A claim, sender cancellation, recipient decline and timeout refund consume **one common escrow nullifier**. This is not implemented by assigning two stock Sapling spending keys to one ordinary note: the conditional relation needs committed, shared nullifier derivation material while separately proving the appropriate branch authority. Public observers learn no resolution-to-opening link from the nullifier; the counterparties may know it, as expected.

Retain the user's **either-side unilateral early-cancel rule** as “the first finalized valid resolution wins.” A recipient's offchain acceptance is pending until its shielded claim is finalized. The sender may validly cancel first; a reorg or censored claim can change the outcome before finality. Requiring both signatures for sender cancellation would change the requested behavior and is therefore only an alternative product policy, not the selected rule.

The timeout is enforced claimability, rather than a guaranteed automatic payment at exactly 24 hours. A wallet or authorized fixed-destination watchtower submits the refund. Prepay its settlement budget so a sender with no remaining ordinary note is not stranded. If the chain halts or inclusion is censored, no circuit can guarantee a wall-clock refund.

Fund settlement fees from a separately committed reserve or additional ordinary inputs, outside the recipient-priced principal. A successful claim pays the full quoted principal to the recipient; a decline, cancellation or timeout returns that principal minus the specified 1% penalty. Specify where any unused fee reserve goes. Otherwise settlement costs could silently change both promised outcomes.

Authenticate creation time and compare it against inclusion-checked validity bounds. Do not reveal each escrow's exact deadline in a unique transaction expiry field: use shared global windows. A circuit can require the claim window to end before its private deadline, and the refund window to start afterward. This gives a bounded near-deadline availability/latency tradeoff; specify it in seconds and test it. It must not be described as exact second-by-second claim availability with no metadata cost.

### 14.4 Fee and supply policy

Use a common disclosed base fee for equivalent envelopes. Enforce the invited/stranger difference privately, using a recipient-bound invitation capability or authenticated price-policy proof. Bind every hidden charge to either a protocol-owned shielded treasury output or a retired-value accounting output according to the fee split. An unconstrained “extra fee” variable would allow a sender to avoid the intended burn or treasury contribution.

For the 1% refund penalty, retire value through a committed, circuit-enforced unspendable note type or an equivalently reviewed confidential supply accumulator. Never publish its amount as a per-transaction issuance decrease. All ordinary and escrow spend branches must reject the retired-value type, including after upgrades unless an explicitly reviewed migration preserves retirement.

**Required interpretation of “public pool”:** its existence, spending policy, governance and accounting commitments can be public while confidential contributions are hidden. A fixed public base-fee share can be credited immediately to a conventional public reserve. The private differential goes into the protocol's shielded treasury, with shielded operator payouts. If the requirement instead demands an exact, immediately public integer balance for every variable contribution, the selected action-hiding policy is incompatible with it. Delayed aggregate disclosure is a possible extension, but it needs a separate aggregation, key-management, liveness and low-volume-leakage design; it is not included as an already solved feature of this recommendation.

Likewise, the publicly identified fee treasury is a system-recipient exception. If “recipient of **any** payment” literally includes the fee pool and validator/staking flows, replace that wording with a scoped user-payment goal or make those flows confidential too. A public treasury cannot simultaneously be an undisclosed recipient.

### 14.5 Why this over the alternatives

- **Versus unchanged Orchard/Ironwood:** its setup-free proof and stronger current assurance tooling are valuable, but its ordinary Action relation does not enforce this escrow. A new relation and runtime integration remain necessary, and the evidence does not establish a phone-plus-stable2609 performance budget.
- **Versus unchanged MASP:** a reusable Rust shielded engine is valuable, but multi-asset conversion, rewards and domain-specific assumptions add machinery for a one-coin chain without resolving conditional escrow.
- **Versus a general PLONK/Noir stack:** programmable circuits and universal SRS reuse are attractive; backend version, ZK flavor, compiler, non-Rust prover components and runtime verification add choices the small team must freeze and audit.
- **Versus a zkVM:** ordinary Rust business logic reduces one development burden while importing a large VM, recursion and wrapper stack. Available hardware evidence is a poor fit for fully local phones; native SP1 proof privacy is also not what the name “zkVM” might suggest.
- **Versus copying a Substrate privacy pallet:** none of the inspected codebases establishes this exact combination of private values, recipients, conditional escrow, no public payer, phone-local proving and current audit coverage. Borrow components and tests, not an inherited security label.

These are architecture judgments dated 2026-11-19. Their factual inputs are the candidate, runtime and implementation tables, rather than a claim of comparative benchmarks conducted here.

**The strongest objection remains new-protocol risk.** A compact Groth16 proof can validly prove an underconstrained statement, and a trusted setup can bind exactly the wrong statement. A small team unable to fund specialist protocol review, independent circuit review, runtime/wallet review and remediation should stop at a no-real-value prototype. The feasibility plan deliberately tests the unsolved parts before a ceremony or public security promise.

## 16. What the finished design will NOT hide

This is the **intended scope and residual-leakage list, dated 2026-11-19**, for the recommended architecture, rather than a claim that an implementation has achieved it.

- The existence, inclusion time, total count and byte sizes of onchain transactions, commitments, nullifiers and proofs.
- Public protocol versions, accepted roots, coarse validity windows, fixed base fees and any deliberately disclosed epoch totals.
- Historical balances, contacts, blocks and channel records already published before the shielded cutover. Deleting current state cannot erase an observer's archive; a public-to-private migration has its own entry linkage.
- The existence and policy of a publicly identified treasury; any balance or payout left deliberately public.
- Individual variable fee contributions from anyone holding the treasury's viewing capability. Such privileged observers may classify contact activity; rotating or threshold-controlling spending keys does not itself remove viewing leakage.
- Transparent deposits/withdrawals, exchange/bridge records, fiat purchases, and timing/amount links at those boundaries.
- Validator identities, block production, public governance and any staking/slashing flows not explicitly redesigned for privacy.
- The public contact profile, advertised contact key and recipient price/policy; how the profile was discovered or shared outside the protected protocol.
- A participant's knowledge of its own counterparty, offer, escrow value, resolution and messages, or disclosures that participant voluntarily makes.
- Wallet compromise, screen capture, malicious updates, insecure backups, exported viewing keys, device identifiers and analytics/notification leakage.
- A gateway's knowledge that a client connects to it, availability/online patterns and network observations outside the bounded mixnet threat model.
- RPC request contents and patterns unless the selected full-scan, bucket or PIR policy actually hides them; routing alone does not hide a query from its destination.
- Timing and participation inferences that remain when very few honest users are active, when the attacker creates known activity, or when all plausible alternatives are excluded by outside information.
- Selective censorship, denial of service, transaction ordering, a halted chain, or receipt before finality.
- Exact real-time refunds without an online wallet/delegate and chain inclusion.
- Past chain ciphertext against future quantum attacks when its encryption suite and public-key exposure do not provide the required post-quantum confidentiality.
- A guarantee of lawful operation, legal immunity, EU listing eligibility, or regulatory acceptance from the presence of viewing keys or association-set proofs.

Padding and private predicates can reduce action classification; they do not hide that money activity occurred. Nym background traffic can help the network anonymity set without creating more independent honest users in this coin's financial pool.

Its central unresolved issue is not whether a SNARK can prove a private transfer; it is whether a small team can deliver and sustain the complete escrow, accounting, wallet and network privacy protocol with reviewable assurance.
