Audit Verdict: Not Safe for Real Value or Production Privacy

The off-chain cryptographic prototype in crates/crypto demonstrates unusually disciplined Rust engineering—strict Ed25519 verification, clean wallet-to-contact key separation, versioned domain tags, and atomic revocation. However, the end-to-end system fails its core anonymity claim today: the live Substrate pallet publishes the entire contact graph in plaintext and contains an ExpiryQueue censorship DoS and cross-chain signature replay, stranger notes carry non-deniable transferable signatures without forward secrecy, and the shielded layer remains an unproved plain-Rust model with trivially distinguishable all-zero dummy payloads.

Plaintext On-Chain State and Non-Deniable Notes Break Core Privacy Goals

Two critical findings directly contradict the target security claim in docs/cryptographer-brief.md—that a chain and network observer cannot determine who is in contact or learn payment metadata. Both must be resolved architecturally before any testnet with real users.

CRIT-01 · CRITICAL

pallet-anonymum/src/lib.rs

shielded-model/src/chain.rs

Complete On-Chain Contact Graph Exposure & Unproved Shielded Layer

The live Substrate pallet stores MessagingKeys, Prices, Invites, Channels, ChannelByPair, Requests, RequestByPair, and Blocks in plaintext on-chain, while crates/shielded-model only simulates shielded state transitions in plain Rust without a ZK circuit, proof verifier, or note encryption. Furthermore, docs/cryptographer-brief.md flags that the target proof system is still unchosen and that Polkadot SDK's RFC-0163 curve host functions do not check subgroup membership on decode—a severe soundness hazard if paired with unaudited arkworks primitives, where invalid-curve points can forge proofs or leak witness scalars.

Remediation: Keep pallet-anonymum strictly as a non-production transparent reference. For the shielded pallet, freeze on a single audited proving stack (e.g., Groth16 over BLS12-381 with a multi-party setup or Halo 2/Pasta with formal constraint auditing in light of the May 2026 Orchard ZIP 257 soundness flaw) and enforce explicit prime-order subgroup validation on every deserialized curve point before any pairing or multi-scalar multiplication.

CRIT-02 · CRITICAL

crates/crypto/src/note.rs

Non-Deniable Transferable Proof of Contact & Zero Forward Secrecy on Stranger Notes

In seal_note, a stranger signs the 443-byte plaintext body with their long-term Ed25519 contact key over b"anonymum/contact-note-signature/v3" || network || admission || recipient_inbox_key || body, then wraps it in a Noise_N_25519_ChaChaPoly_BLAKE2s envelope addressed to the recipient's static inbox key. Because Ed25519 is publicly verifiable, any recipient—or any adversary who compromises the recipient's inbox key—gains a cryptographically undeniable, third-party transferable proof that sender attempted to contact recipient_inbox_key with a specific intro and admission. Worse, Noise N offers no forward secrecy for the recipient: compromising a long-lived inbox secret retroactively decrypts every historical stranger note ever sealed to it.

Remediation: Replace the Ed25519 signature inside stranger notes with deniable authentication—such as a Noise IK/K triple-DH MAC or a designated-verifier ring signature over {sender_pk, recipient_inbox_pk}—so the recipient can verify authorship while being able to forge identical transcripts themselves. Enforce short-lived epoch rotation on ContactCard.inbox_key (via tight not_after bounds) and securely zeroize expired inbox private keys.

Substrate Pallet Vulnerabilities Enable Censorship DoS and Cross-Chain Replay

Even within its scope as a transparent chain prototype, pallet-anonymum contains three exploitable runtime flaws that affect liveness, signature integrity, and arithmetic safety.

Deterministic ExpiryQueue Exhaustion (HIGH-01)

request_contact calculates a deterministic expiry block (block_number() + request_ttl_blocks()) and appends the request ID into ExpiryQueue[expires_at], a BoundedVec capped at MaxRequestsPerBlock. Crucially, early resolutions—accept, decline, cancel, supersede, and block—never remove the ID from ExpiryQueue. An attacker can submit MaxRequestsPerBlock self-requests in a single block (immediately cancelling or accepting them to recycle funds), permanently filling that expiry slot and censoring every legitimate request_contact call in that block. Meanwhile, on_initialize charges only DB read/write weight with zero compute ref_time for up to MaxRequestsPerBlock refund iterations.

Remediation: Replace on_initialize batch expiry with permissionless lazy expiry (an expire_request(id) extrinsic callable after expires_at, incentivized by a small keeper slice of the deposit) and benchmark full Substrate weights.

Cross-Chain Replay & Ed25519 Canonicality Split (HIGH-04)

publish_messaging_key verifies a signature over b"anonymum/msgkey-binding/v1" || key with no AccountId, no chain/genesis hash, and no nonce. Similarly, open_with_invite verifies sp_io::crypto::ed25519_verify over b"anonymum/invite/v1" || opener || recipient with no chain or pallet domain separation. Signatures issued on a testnet or fork remain valid on mainnet. In addition, sp_io::crypto::ed25519_verify accepts non-canonical Ed25519 encodings that ed25519-dalek::verify_strict in crates/crypto rejects—creating a consensus-vs-client validation split.

Remediation: Bind genesis_hash (or network_id), pallet ID, AccountId, and a monotonic nonce or expiry into both signed payloads, and enforce strict RFC 8032 / ZIP 215 canonicality on-chain.

Unchecked Runtime Arithmetic (MED-02)

pay_network_fee computes fee * percent / 100u32.into() using unchecked multiplication (wrapping silently in release builds whenever fee > Balance::MAX / 100 and under-burning fees), while request_contact uses unchecked + for expires_at and take_id()/take_seq() use unchecked *n += 1.

Remediation: Use Percent::from_percent(p).mul_floor(fee), saturating_add, and checked_add returning ArithmeticError::Overflow throughout the pallet.

Four subtler implementation details in this matrix merit close attention during refactoring:

Mixnet tag rotation amplifies state-table DoS (HIGH-02): In wire.rs, Responder keys both pending and sessions by the transport handle T—which in crates/nym/src/main.rs is Nym's AnonymousSenderTag. Because SURB sender tags rotate across batches, confirm()'s pending.retain(|(t, _), _| *t != from) misses earlier pending handshakes from the same caller under prior tags, while sessions has no cap for 30 minutes and every incoming frame triggers an O(pending + sessions) linear scan. Keying sessions by authenticated Noise static public key (updating the active SURB handle in-place) eliminates both memory growth and per-caller lockout.

All-zero dummy payloads break transaction uniformity (MED-03): In shielded-model/src/build.rs, unused encrypted payload slots default to all-zero 252-byte arrays, while real stranger notes in note.rs are 587 bytes. Any passive observer can immediately partition Open transactions from Transfer, Claim, and Decline transactions by inspecting payload entropy—and correlate immediate reverse-rendezvous mixnet bursts in crates/nym against the on-chain confirmation block.

Unchecked nonce increment & volatile revocation (MED-01, MED-04): In session.rs, Session::seal executes unchecked self.next_send += 1 without a hard rekey ceiling; wrapping in release builds would catastrophically reuse nonce 0 under ChaCha20-Poly1305. Meanwhile, gate.rs tracks revoked invitation IDs and callback deadlines in volatile memory using Instant, so a client restart wipes revocation history and allows previously consumed or cancelled invitations to be replayed.

“A messaging coin’s anonymity is bounded by its leakiest layer: airtight Noise IK handshakes cannot protect users if the chain publishes their contact graph or dummy payloads are all zeroes.”

What the Codebase Already Gets Right

Despite the prototype gaps above, several foundational cryptographic choices in crates/crypto and crates/shielded-model reflect strong security discipline worth preserving:

Clean Wallet-to-Contact Key Isolation

Identity (wallet keys) and ContactIdentity (signing, handshake, and inbox keys) are generated independently with zero cross-use, preventing mixnet traffic from linking directly to wallet identities.

Strict Ed25519 & Versioned Domain Tags

Every signature verification in crates/crypto uses verify_strict, and every signed record, Noise prologue, wire marker, and shielded-model Blake2s hash uses explicit versioned domain separation (with u64 LE length-prefixing in hash.rs).

Authorise-Before-Replying & Atomic Revocation

Gate silently drops unauthorised callers (zero oracle leakage), and Responder::block / remove_contact atomically purge live sessions, pending handshakes, cached replies, and unused invitations in one step.

Post-Auth Replay Window & Zeroization

Session::open_if advances its 2,048-message sliding replay window only after AEAD decryption and application-level accept both succeed, and StaticKey wraps private material in Zeroizing<[u8; 32]>.
